# How to Drive Woodward

Woodward works best when the request includes the minimum briefing context that lets him choose the right research mode.

## The four things to specify

### 1. What kind of work is this?
Choose one:
- company diligence
- pathway/technology assessment
- market map
- meeting brief
- claim verification
- standards/policy scan

### 2. What is the decision context?
Explain:
- why you care
- what decision or conversation this informs
- whether you need speed or rigor
- whether this is exploratory or decision-critical

Examples:
- I need a fast but skeptical first-pass on this startup before a meeting.
- I need a deeper diligence brief because this may shape BELT research direction.

### 3. What output shape do you want?
Examples:
- bullet brief
- meeting prep memo
- source-ranked diligence note
- red-flags-only pass
- compare-three-companies matrix
- claim-check memo

### 4. Do you want canon updates?
Say whether Woodward should capture reusable lessons into the climate-tech canon.

## Example prompts

### Company diligence
"Woodward, do company diligence on this climate-tech startup. Decision context: I have a meeting tomorrow and need a fast but skeptical first-pass. Output: bullet brief plus top red flags. Canon updates: yes, if you find reusable diligence heuristics."

### Pathway assessment
"Woodward, assess this biochar pathway. Decision context: this may shape BELT research direction, so prioritize rigor over speed. Output: source-ranked diligence note. Canon updates: yes."

### Claim verification
"Woodward, verify this carbon-removal claim. Decision context: I need to know whether it is safe to repeat publicly. Output: claim-check memo. Canon updates: only if the research reveals a reusable MRV or sourcing lesson."

### Market map
"Woodward, map the competitive landscape around X. Decision context: exploratory, medium rigor. Output: compare-three-companies matrix plus category notes. Canon updates: yes."

## When Woodward should ask follow-up questions

Woodward should ask follow-up questions when the request is missing one of the critical inputs above and the missing piece materially changes the research approach.

He should not over-interview the user if enough is already obvious.

## Best practice

If you are in a hurry, even a compressed request helps a lot:

"Woodward, company diligence, fast first pass, before a meeting, bullet brief, canon updates yes."

That is enough for him to get moving with the right posture.
