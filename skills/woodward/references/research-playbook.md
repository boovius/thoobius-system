# Woodward Research Playbook

## Source quality tiers

### Tier 1, strongest
- official documents
- original datasets
- court filings
- regulatory filings
- company financial filings
- direct transcripts
- published papers / preprints from identifiable authors
- first-party product/docs pages when used carefully

### Tier 2
- credible journalism with named sourcing and specific evidence
- reputable trade publications
- strong analyst or industry reports with transparent methodology

### Tier 3
- company blogs, marketing pages, investor decks
- newsletters and Substacks
- expert commentary without primary evidence attached

### Tier 4, weak
- scraped summaries
- AI sludge
- thin affiliate pages
- content farms
- repeated articles all citing each other

## Claim-checking workflow

For important claims:
1. state the exact claim
2. find the closest primary source
3. find one or two independent corroborating sources if possible
4. note any contradictions
5. assign a confidence level:
   - high
   - medium
   - low

## Synthesis pattern

Use this pattern when reporting back:
- strongest confirmed facts first
- then the best-supported interpretation
- then unresolved ambiguity
- then next-step research recommendations

## Common research task shapes

### Company / startup diligence
Look for:
- what the company says it does
- what independent sources say it does
- funding, customers, pilots, partners
- team background
- signs of technical substance versus narrative inflation
- missing evidence

### Technology / scientific topic
Look for:
- core mechanism
- maturity level
- real constraints
- cost / scale bottlenecks
- who credible experts are
- where the marketing story outruns the evidence

### Policy / regulation
Look for:
- actual statutory or regulatory text
- agency guidance
- implementation date and scope
- who is covered
- known ambiguities and legal disputes

### People / background research
Prefer:
- official bios
- authored work
- speeches, interviews, publications
- repeated patterns across roles and organizations

Avoid speculative character judgments.

## Meeting briefing format

When preparing a briefing, aim for:
- who they are
- what matters about them
- likely overlap with user goals
- strongest conversation hooks
- open questions worth asking
- cautions / unknowns

## Red flags

Flag these clearly:
- circular sourcing
- inconsistent dates or numbers
- unverifiable market-size claims
- “stealth” companies with no substance beyond decks
- scientific claims without methods or provenance
- copied wording across supposedly independent sources
