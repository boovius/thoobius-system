- BELT product vision sharpened further around a wizard-style intake as the near-term wedge between vague ideation and rigorous LCA. Core framing: BELT should help users turn rough waste-to-value concepts into structured scenarios by asking goal-and-scope questions, surfacing assumptions, clarifying process structure, and judging whether deeper analysis is warranted.
- Durable product-model insight: treat scenario structure and assumptions/readiness as two separate but linked layers. Scenario side is effectively the inventory representation made legible; assumptions are a first-class overlay with provenance, confidence, scope, type, value, and rationale rather than hidden defaults or loose notes.
- Strong architectural recommendation captured for BELT issue #31: use a declarative wizard-step layer, an intermediate ScenarioDraft/OpportunityDraft model, a separate assumptions/defaults enrichment pass with provenance (`user_entered`, `defaulted`, `inferred`), a split between basic validation and higher-level readiness diagnostics, and an explicit draft-to-scenario translator.
- BELT issue #31 created: `Product: wizard-style goal-and-scope intake for early scenario definition` — https://github.com/boovius/belt/issues/31
- Kaylee reviewed BELT branch `scenario-inapp-authoring` and clarified the intended architecture: wizard/intake for rough opportunity framing -> translator/scaffold -> in-app authoring for refining the canonical runnable scenario. Important schema boundary: intake-only concepts like confidence, unknowns, maturity, and provenance should remain outside the canonical scenario schema in a separate intake draft/metadata layer.
- Durable modeling direction for BELT: a scenario may contain multiple higher-level workflow units (possibly called Activity Groups / Workflow Blocks rather than Transformations), each containing ordered stages, with one or more processes inside a stage. Stages are sequential; processes in a stage may be conceptually parallel/co-occurring. Keep the model minimal and avoid overbuilding into a general process-simulation engine too early.
