- Ran Woodward’s first structured research pass on: energy usage to dry seaweed for use as pyrolysis feedstock. User’s briefing: pathway/technology assessment; goal is assessing energy needs/requirements to estimate cost and carbon footprint and judge business viability; desired output was a source-ranked diligence note; canon updates yes; source tracking yes.
- Woodward’s first-pass conclusion: drying is likely the make-or-break viability constraint for seaweed-to-pyrolysis pathways. Business plausibility depends heavily on starting moisture, target moisture, drying method, climate, throughput, and access to passive dewatering, solar drying, waste heat, or process-heat integration. Strong caution against assuming the downstream pyrolysis/LCA benefits matter unless upstream drying is modeled honestly.
- Sources cited in the seaweed drying pass included: PubMed/Water Research 2022 on engineered beach-cast seaweed pyrolysis and LCA (`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35870392/`), a PMC article on convective drying of brown seaweed with reported specific energy consumption of 18.29 to 25.25 kWh per kg of water removed (`https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12691876/`), a Springer article snippet suggesting ~10 kWh per kg of dry seaweed produced (`https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10499-025-01978-x`), and a ScienceDirect review on seaweed drying approaches/challenges (`https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924224423001723`).
- Reusable canon lesson from this pass: upstream drying can dominate biomass/seaweed pathway realism, and food-grade drying numbers may overstate what pyrolysis-feedstock preparation strictly requires.
